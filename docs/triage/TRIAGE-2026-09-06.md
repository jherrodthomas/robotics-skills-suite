## Pair-edition audit — re-run, and a false-positive class found

`scripts/audit_pair_editions.py`: **38 pairs scanned · 1 mismatch · 10 asymmetries** — unchanged
from 2026-09-03, as expected on a no-polish day.

The useful result is negative. The 2026-09-03 follow-up asked the next run to `grep` the suite
for `Z490.1-2016` before pinning anything in the asymmetry slot. **That grep hits, and the hit is
a false positive.** `operator-training-matrix-checklist-reviewer` line 45 reads:

> A citation of ANSI/RIA R15.06-2012, ISO 10218-2:2011 or Z490.1-2016 as the governing edition is
> a finding.

The superseded edition is named *as the thing to reject*. The same pattern explains the
`declaration-of-conformity` asymmetry, where the reviewer "pins" ANSI R15.06-2012 only inside a
list of editions that must not be cited, while the builder does not pin R15.06 at all.

**Neither the grep nor `audit_pair_editions.py` distinguishes a citation from a prohibition.**
Both will keep reporting a well-written reviewer as carrying a stale edition, and the failure
direction is the dangerous one — it makes correct files look broken, so a future run may "fix"
a reviewer by deleting the very sentence that rejects the superseded edition. Before the W37
asymmetry slot runs, the audit needs a negative-context filter (skip a year that appears within
a sentence containing *is a finding*, *must not*, *superseded*, *rather than*), and the
`declaration-of-conformity` row should be re-read by hand rather than auto-pinned.
