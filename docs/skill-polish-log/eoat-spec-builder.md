**What's good**

**What to fix**
1. **Zero standards references in either half (primary, and the worst instance found in the suite so far).** Not stale editions — *none at all*. Every other cell-design and compliance builder polished since 2026-07 carries an edition-anchored `## Standards` section; this pair had no section and no standard named anywhere, in a safety-critical domain. A reviewer built from it could not flag a spec citing the withdrawn ISO 9409-1:1996 or the superseded ISO 10218:2011, because it had no baseline to compare against.
2. **"Payload" treated as a scalar mass.** The single largest real-world defect in EOAT specs. A robot wrist is bounded by four independent limits — mass, CoG offset, allowable moments, and mass moment of inertia — and inertia is the field most often left blank while being the one that actually caps acceleration. The builder said "payload" and "payload masses" (twice, redundantly) and nothing about offset, moments or inertia.
3. **Grip retention on loss of energy entirely absent.** This is the ISO 10218-2 requirement the end-effector clause exists for, and the reason an EOAT spec is a safety document rather than a procurement document. Neither half mentioned it. The restore direction (no unexpected release or re-grip on power restoration) is the more commonly missed half and was likewise absent.
4. **Tool changer compatibility asserted with no interface grammar.** "Tool changer compatibility" is not checkable without the ISO 9409-1 designation on both sides. Nothing carried pitch circle diameter, hole count, thread size or plate type, so compatibility was a prose claim.
5. **No regulatory-status decision.** Whether an EOAT is covered by the cell's declaration or is interchangeable equipment needing its own CE marking under EU 2023/1230 determines who signs what — and the repo already has `declaration-of-conformity-builder` waiting downstream. The question was not asked.
6. **No `## Related skills` section**, so the pair sat outside the chain despite having obvious upstream (`robot-cell-scope`, `iso12100-risk-assessment`, `operating-envelope`) and downstream (`safety-io-matrix`, `iso13849-plr`, `declaration-of-conformity`) neighbours.
7. **Reviewer had no pass-fail criteria despite the description promising "structured pass-fail criteria".** Four bullet headings and nothing under them. Same chain-break shape corrected in `robot-hil-test-catalog` (2026-08-18) and `robot-acceptance-protocol` (2026-08-12).

**Edition verification** (cell-design is in the mandatory safety-critical set; every citation here is new to this pair, so all were checked rather than carried)
- **ISO 9409-1:2004** confirmed current on iso.org (std 36578) and the ANSI webstore; the **1996 edition (std 20624) is withdrawn**. Recorded the withdrawn edition in the reviewer's baseline so it can be flagged. Also captured the standard's own scope limit — it defines the plate, *not* the coupling device, and carries **no** correlation to load-carrying capacity. That limit is load-bearing for this skill: it is why a matching flange designation can never substitute for the payload/moment budget, and it is written into the builder as such.
- **ISO 14539:2000** confirmed on iso.org (std 24062) as Edition 1, **last reviewed and confirmed 2021**, currently at stage 90.20 (under systematic review). Not withdrawn. Flagged in the builder as a vocabulary standard, not a safety standard — it exists so two suppliers' datasheets compare, and should not be cited as if it carried requirements.
- **ISO 4414:2010** confirmed Edition 2 on iso.org (std 44790); harmonised under the Machinery Directive since 8 April 2011. New citation to this suite. Scoped correctly in the builder: it covers the tool's pneumatic circuit and explicitly excludes factory air distribution and compressors, so it is not a licence to specify the plant supply from this workbook.
- **Regulation (EU) 2023/1230** — applies **20 January 2027**, repeals 2006/42/EC. Confirmed that interchangeable equipment is a named Annex product category alongside machinery, safety components and partly completed machinery, which is the hook for the regulatory-status section. Matches the phrasing already carried by `machinery-safety-lifecycle-plan` and `declaration-of-conformity`.
- ISO 10218-1:2025 (Ed 3) / ISO 10218-2:2025 (Ed 2), ISO 13849-1:2023 (Ed 5), ISO 13849-2:2012 (Ed 2, DIS in enquiry), ISO 12100:2010, IEC 60204-1:2016, ISO/TS 15066:2016 carried from prior verified passes — unchanged, not re-litigated. The do-not-bump note on ISO/DIS 13849-2 propagated into both halves, consistent with `robot-hil-test-catalog` and `safety-io-matrix`.
